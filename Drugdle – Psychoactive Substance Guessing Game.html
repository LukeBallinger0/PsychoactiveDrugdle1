How the categories are defined

Effect: sedating or stimulating effect on a five-step scale: very sedating, mildly sedating, neutral, mildly stimulating, very stimulating. It applies to every drug, so psychedelics and dissociatives are placed too (e.g. ketamine sedating, LSD mildly stimulating). Hallucinogenic: not, mildly (e.g. MDMA, cannabis, DXM) or very (classic psychedelics, dissociatives, deliriants). Molecule size: small (below ~250 g/mol), medium (~250–340) or large (above ~340). Basic nitrogen: contains an amine-type nitrogen that can be protonated. Analogue: a modified or designer derivative of an established parent compound, such as substituted tryptamines and phenethylamines (4-HO-MET, 2C-I), cathinones, novel dissociatives (3-MeO-PCP) and GABA analogues. The parent drugs themselves (amphetamine, methamphetamine, PCP, DMT, LSD and so on) are not analogues. Addiction: low, medium or high potential. Duration: total effect length (<1h, 1–6h, 6–12h+). Chemical class is the structural family (e.g. morphinan, benzodiazepine, barbiturate, tryptamine). Mechanism is the main pharmacological action, shown as an extra clue.

Values were compiled from general knowledge of PsychonautWiki-style profiles and are simplified for gameplay. Check psychonautwiki.org for full details. For education only, not drug-use advice.
